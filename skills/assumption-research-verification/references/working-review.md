# Reviewing a working model

Read the user's goal and adopted interpretation rules, then the selected model and
its grounds. Ask whether this is the most reasonable useful picture of the available
subject, not whether every normal inference has a separately signed document.

Check that entities are identified consistently, new facts add diagnostic grounds,
copies and circular inferences do not reinforce themselves, meaningful contradictions
are considered, and the selected relations form a coherent picture. Check normal
operation and outcomes as well as anomalies. A reviewer may propose a better model
or a concrete observation that would materially change the interpretation.

User conventions are premises of the task. Under an adopted unit-interpretation
rule, missing extra paperwork is not a defect. A documented different scale
can be a real contradiction. Ordinary interpretations of recording patterns can
remain plausible without a separate certificate; exact parameters and identities
still need their own basis or a stated default.

Do not require new external cases or independent datasets merely to reconstruct one
subject. Distinct observations within that subject can reinforce its model. A test
of statistical/general population claims may need different evidence, when that is
actually the user's question.

Review feedback is attributed judgment. Use `reasonable` for a coherent working
picture with ordinary assumptions and bounded residual questions; use `revise` for
concrete semantic mistakes, ignored contradictions or material omitted parts of the
goal. Identify the relevant claim and source/rule. An optional review does not turn
the output into a universally proven description of reality.
